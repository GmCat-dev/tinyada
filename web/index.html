    Why TinyADA?

    🛡

    Typo protection

    Most crypto addresses carry a built-in check. TinyADA tests it before making a link, so a mistyped address never gets shared.

    👀

    Ending check

    The link page shows the last characters in big letters, so the person paying can compare them with their wallet. A simple defence against address-swapping malware.

    💸

    Payment requests

    Add an amount and a note (“Coffee for GmCat, 5 ADA”). On a phone, one tap opens the payer’s wallet with everything filled in.

    🏷

    Cardano $handles

    Type $yourname instead of an address. TinyADA finds the address the handle points to, today and every time the link is opened.

    ▦

    QR codes

    Every link comes with a QR code you can download, ready for a poster, a stream or a market stall.

    🐾

    Free and private

    No ads, no account, no tracking. TinyADA keeps the address, your note and a visit count. That’s it.

    Back after a little nap

    TinyADA started in 2019 as TinyADA.com, a tiny-URL maker for crypto addresses made by GmCat’s creator. It shortened Bitcoin, Litecoin, Ethereum and Cardano addresses, and the plan was always to add custom names next. Then the server ran out of room and the site took a long nap.     Good to know

    Is TinyADA free?

    Yes. Making links, QR codes and payment requests is free, with no ads and no account.

    How long do links last?

    You choose: 1 day, 7 days, 30 days, or keep while in use. A “keep while in use” link stays as long as people open it, and is deleted after 6 months without a single visit. Each visit restarts the clock.

    Why not keep free links forever?

    Most links are shared once and never opened again. Free links have no account, so nobody could ever tidy them up. “Keep while in use” keeps the links people really use and clears the forgotten ones by itself.

    Can I change the address on a link?

    Not on a free link: a free link always shows the address it was made with, which keeps it trustworthy. Make a new link instead. With TinyADA Plus you can edit your links, and visitors see when the address changed.

    Is it safe to pay from a TinyADA link?

    TinyADA checks every address for typos, but it can’t know who owns it. Only pay people you trust, and always compare the ending on the link page with the address your wallet shows. See something fishy? Use “Report” on the link page.

    I made a link for the same address twice. Why is it the same link?

    On purpose. One address (with the same amount and note) always gets one free link, so nobody can fill TinyADA with thousands of copies. Asking again just keeps the link at least as long as you asked.

    What does GmCat store?

    The address, the amount and note you add, when the link was made and last opened, and a visit count. No IP addresses and no cookies for free links. Each link’s owner key stays in your browser (we keep only a scrambled fingerprint of it).

    Can I upgrade a link without an account?

    Yes, with Privacy Plus : open the link and choose “Upgrade this link”. Keep it up for a year (anyone can pay for that, so fans can keep a creator’s link alive), turn it into a tip page you can edit, add visit counts, or give it a custom name. You prove the link is yours with its owner key, or by signing with the wallet that holds the address. The wallet always wins.
